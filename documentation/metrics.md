# Metric Definitions

This document defines the metrics used in the BigQuery ecommerce analysis. These definitions ensure that calculations remain consistent across SQL queries and future dashboards.

## Data Sources

### Orders

Source: `bigquery-public-data.thelook_ecommerce.orders`

Grain: One row per order.

### Order Items

Source: `bigquery-public-data.thelook_ecommerce.order_items`

Grain: One row per item within an order.

### Products

Source: `bigquery-public-data.thelook_ecommerce.products`

Grain: One row per product.

Join relationship:

`order_items.product_id = products.id`

### Users

Source: `bigquery-public-data.thelook_ecommerce.users`

Grain: One row per registered customer.

Join relationship:

`order_items.user_id = users.id`

## Core Metrics

### Gross Item Value

The total sale price of all order items, regardless of order status.

Formula:

`SUM(sale_price)`

This metric is useful for initial exploration but should not be treated as recognized revenue because it includes cancelled, returned, processing, and shipped items.

### Recognized Revenue

The total sale price of items that have a status of `Complete` and a non-null delivery timestamp.

Formula:

`SUM(sale_price)`

Filters:

- `status = 'Complete'`
- `delivered_at IS NOT NULL`

Revenue is assigned to the month in which the item was delivered.

### Completed Orders

The number of distinct orders containing completed items with a non-null delivery timestamp.

Formula:

`COUNT(DISTINCT order_id)`

### Completed Items

The number of completed order-item rows with a non-null delivery timestamp.

Formula:

`COUNT(*)`

### Average Order Value

The average recognized revenue generated by each completed order.

Formula:

`recognized revenue / completed orders`

The SQL uses `SAFE_DIVIDE` to prevent division-by-zero errors.

### Estimated Gross Profit

The difference between recognized revenue and product cost for completed items.

Formula:

`SUM(sale_price - product cost)`

This is not net company profit because it excludes shipping, taxes, payroll, marketing, and other operating expenses.

### Gross Margin Percentage

The percentage of recognized revenue remaining after product cost.

Formula:

`estimated gross profit / recognized revenue × 100`

## Reporting Period

All reporting views include completed calendar months only. The current incomplete month is excluded so monthly revenue, category performance, and customer segment metrics use the same reporting period.

## Monthly Trend Metrics

### Previous Month Revenue

The recognized revenue from the preceding monthly row.

The SQL uses `LAG(recognized_revenue)` with the results ordered by `revenue_month`.

The first month has no previous month, so its value is `NULL`.

### Revenue Change

The absolute increase or decrease in recognized revenue compared with the previous month.

Formula:

`current month revenue - previous month revenue`

A positive value represents an increase, while a negative value represents a decrease.

### Revenue Growth Percentage

The percentage change in recognized revenue compared with the previous month.

Formula:

`(current month revenue - previous month revenue) / previous month revenue × 100`

The SQL uses `SAFE_DIVIDE` to prevent division-by-zero errors.

### Year-over-Year Revenue Change

The absolute change in recognized revenue compared with the same calendar month
one year earlier.

Formula:

`current month revenue - revenue 12 months earlier`

### Year-over-Year Growth Percentage

The percentage change in recognized revenue compared with the same calendar
month one year earlier.

Formula:

`(current month revenue - revenue 12 months earlier) / revenue 12 months earlier × 100`

The first 12 months are `NULL` because no prior-year comparison exists.

### Rolling 3-Month Revenue

The sum of recognized revenue for the current calendar month and the preceding
two calendar months. The first two rows are `NULL` until a complete three-month
window exists.

### Rolling 3-Month Average Revenue

The rolling 3-month revenue divided by three.

### Rolling 12-Month Revenue

The sum of recognized revenue for the current calendar month and the preceding
11 calendar months. The first 11 rows are `NULL` until a complete 12-month
window exists.

### Rolling 12-Month Average Revenue

The rolling 12-month revenue divided by 12.

### Calendar Continuity

The monthly revenue growth view generates a calendar containing every month
between the first and latest completed delivery month. Months with no completed
activity receive zero orders, zero items, and zero recognized revenue. This
ensures that month-over-month, year-over-year, and rolling calculations use
calendar periods rather than merely the previous available data row.

## Category Comparison Metrics

### Revenue Rank

The position of each product category when categories are ordered from highest to lowest recognized revenue.

The SQL uses:

`RANK() OVER (ORDER BY recognized_revenue DESC)`

A revenue rank of `1` identifies the category with the highest recognized revenue.

### Revenue Share Percentage

The percentage of total recognized revenue contributed by a product category.

Formula:

`category recognized revenue / total recognized revenue × 100`

The revenue-share percentages across all categories should total approximately `100%`. Small differences can occur because displayed values are rounded.

## Customer Segments

Customer segments are based on the number of distinct completed orders associated with each registered customer.

### No Completed Purchases

A registered customer with zero completed orders.

This segment can include customers with no orders and customers whose orders have only non-complete statuses.

### One-Time Customer

A registered customer with exactly one completed order.

### Repeat Customer

A registered customer with more than one completed order.

### Average Orders per Customer

The average number of completed orders generated by customers within a segment.

Formula:

`segment completed orders / customers in the segment`

### Average Revenue per Customer

The average recognized revenue generated by customers within a segment.

Formula:

`segment recognized revenue / customers in the segment`

## Interpretation Rules

- Order counts calculated separately by category must not be added together because one order can contain products from multiple categories.
- Item counts, revenue, and estimated gross profit can be added across mutually exclusive product categories.
- Customer segmentation includes all registered users because the analysis starts with the `users` table and uses a left join.
- The `monthly_revenue_growth` view generates missing calendar months before applying window functions, so monthly comparisons always use consecutive calendar periods.
- Growth percentages should be interpreted together with absolute revenue changes. A small comparison-period revenue base can produce a large percentage movement.
- Financial values are converted to BigQuery `NUMERIC` before aggregation to reduce floating-point precision issues.
- These definitions are project assumptions and should be confirmed with business stakeholders in a production environment.
